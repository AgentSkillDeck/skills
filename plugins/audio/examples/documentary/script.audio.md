[scene: 1. The problem]
[ambience: wind | low]
NARRATOR (calm): In the early eighteen hundreds, a lighthouse was a fire in a box. Most of its light went up into the sky, or down into the rocks.
NARRATOR (authoritative): Ships could not see it until they were already close. Too close, on a stormy night.
[scene: 2. The idea]
[ambience: wind | low]
NARRATOR: In eighteen twenty-two, a French engineer named Augustin Fresnel showed a different lens. Not one thick slab of glass, but rings of thin prisms, stepped like a staircase.
NARRATOR (surprised): Each ring bent the light the same way. Together they threw almost all of it toward the sea.
[scene: 3. The night it worked]
[ambience: wind | low]
KEEPER (tender): The first night we lit it, the beam reached the far headland. We just stood there and watched it turn.
NARRATOR (calm): A year later, the lens was shining from the Cordouan lighthouse. Within a few decades, lights like it guarded coasts around the world.
